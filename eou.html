Frequently Asked Questions

Common EOU Queries Answered

The 100% Export Oriented Unit (EOU) scheme is a government initiative under the Foreign Trade Policy that allows manufacturing units to operate with significant duty benefits — including exemption from Customs duty on import of capital goods, raw materials, and consumables — provided the unit exports its entire production (excluding permitted DTA sales). The scheme is governed by the Development Commissioner and requires compliance with both Customs and Central Excise regulations, including registration, bond execution, and periodic return filing.

EOU units must maintain compliance on multiple fronts: registration with the Development Commissioner and dual registration under Customs and Central Excise, execution of B/17 bond, obtaining a warehousing license, filing monthly returns with Customs and Central Excise authorities, submitting quarterly progress reports and annual statements to the Development Commissioner, maintaining proper records of imports and exports, obtaining permissions for DTA sales and procurement from the domestic tariff area, and meeting the stipulated export obligation. Non-compliance can lead to show cause notices, penalties, or even cancellation of the EOU status.
